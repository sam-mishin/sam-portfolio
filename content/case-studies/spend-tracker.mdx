<CaseSection id="customer">

The owner and general manager of 14 Acres Vineyard & Winery, a working winery and tasting room in Ridgefield, Washington. Every sale goes through the **Clover POS**, but there was no single view of revenue and the top-selling food and wine.

The GM uses the dashboard once or twice a week to see a breakdown of each member: their favorite wines, how much they’ve spent on wine vs. food, their favorite day, how many comped tastings they’ve been given, and more.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="discovery">

**The real problem:** the GM needed accurate data on which wine club members take advantage of their comped tastings, and which don’t.

**What it grew into:** each member’s favorite wines and food, their lifetime spend and average order size, and all customers, not just members.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="constraints">

- **Existing systems:** the data lives in Clover, so the tracker reads orders from the Clover API rather than asking staff to enter anything.
- **Volume:** the sync now holds 50,000+ orders, so it has to keep up night after night.
- **Duplicate customers:** Clover has lots of duplicate customer records, so I had to design a way for several Clover customers to link to the same profile.
- **Orders with no customer:** plenty of orders have no customer attached, and they can’t be thrown out.

</CaseSection>

A nightly sync copies Clover POS orders into Postgres (a Supabase function on a Vercel Cron), and on top of it sit a filter-and-search page and dashboards showing different totals and trends.

Three decisions shaped it:

- **A nightly sync, not live data.** The GM doesn’t need live numbers, and a nightly sync is more efficient.
- **Several Clover customers, one profile.** Duplicate customer records link to a single profile, so one person’s spend isn’t split across records.
- **Every order counts.** Orders with no customer attached are kept, so revenue totals stay complete.

<CaseSection id="rollout">

Training was simple: the whole site is one filter-and-search page plus dashboards showing different totals and trends.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="results">

- **50,000+ orders** synced from Clover into Postgres so far.

- The GM checks it once or twice a week for accurate data on which members use their comped tastings.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="broke">

**Duplicate customers.** Duplicate Clover customer records — the same person saved more than once — split one customer’s spend across several records. The fix was letting several Clover customers link to the same account. I’m also building a hand-written name-similarity matcher, tested with Vitest, and redesigning the sync around webhooks.

**Orders with no customer.** I built the tracker assuming every order had a customer attached, so at first the code ignored orders without one. That made total revenue for different food items wrong, so I changed it to keep every order.
