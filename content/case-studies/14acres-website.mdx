<CaseSection id="customer">

14 Acres Vineyard & Winery is a working winery, tasting room, and event venue in Ridgefield, Washington, open Wednesday through Sunday. **14acres.com** gets **100–300 visitors a day**: people planning a visit, guests checking the event calendar, wine club members, and couples and companies asking about private events.

Every piece of marketing there has the same goal: get people into the tasting room first, and into the wine club second. The website is where most of that lands.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="discovery">

**What they had:** a buggy WordPress site, and two tasting room TVs that played the same looping video from a USB media player.

**What the site had to do:** the big one was letting staff keep the event calendar up to date without a developer. Inquiries also had to reach the right inbox, the site had to check that visitors are 21 or older, and the QR codes on the brochure and in the tasting room had to open something useful on a phone.

**The real problem:** events change every week. I wanted each event to live in one CMS item, so one update would reach the event pages, the TVs, and the AI receptionist’s event info all at once.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="constraints">

I tried to keep custom scripts to a minimum so the site stays fast, which is why tracking goes through Google Tag Manager and not Webflow custom code. There’s also a 21+ age gate on every page, and each form asks for email and SMS marketing consent as two separate yes/no questions.

Staff add events and wines themselves in the Webflow CMS, so nothing they edit should ever need code. Every change goes to the Webflow staging site before it goes to the live domain. On the hardware side, both TVs run off one HDMI splitter, driven by a small Dell OptiPlex that stays on 24/7. It also runs karaoke night.

</CaseSection>

I rebuilt the site in Webflow and launched it at the end of August 2026.

<Decisions>
  <Decision title="CMS collections, not pages">
    Upcoming Events, Wines, Summer Concerts, and TV Promos are collections. Weekly events are a single item with a recurring switch (“every Wednesday”) rather than one item per date.
  </Decision>
  <Decision title="Empty fields disappear">
    Event pages hide any row whose field is empty, and show “Reserve your seats” only when there’s a registration link, using Webflow’s own conditional visibility, not custom CSS.
  </Decision>
  <Decision title="One event, two images">
    Every event has two images: a 4:3 card (1600×1200) and a wide hero (2400×1000), both WebP and under 1 MB. When a photo doesn’t crop cleanly, I swap it for a different one rather than squeezing it.
  </Decision>
</Decisions>

A few other pieces went into the build. The 21+ age gate is a small script in the site’s head that remembers each visitor’s answer in a cookie. It has to load before the scroll-reveal script, so I pinned it above it.

For wines, there’s an out-of-stock toggle that adds a badge and dims the card on the wine list, the wine page, and the food menu pairings. Member prices are calculated to the exact cent (20% off), and the homepage links open the wine list already filtered by type, like red or white.

The QR hub (/hub) is one mobile page for the brochure and tasting room QR codes, with ten actions: events, registration, wine club, reservations (a tap-to-call link), private events, hours and directions, menu, social, newsletter, and a Google review link. I built it from the site’s existing Webflow classes and named any new ones in Finsweet Client-First.

The site has four lead forms (contact, wine club, tours, and event inquiries). Every field on them is required, and email and SMS consent are separate yes/no questions. For tracking, Google Tag Manager and GA4 run across the whole site, and clicks on the directions, phone, and event buttons count as conversions.

For the TVs, the OptiPlex runs a rolling slideshow of upcoming events pulled from the CMS, and each slide has a QR code that goes back to the website. After every couple of events, a promo slide plays. The promo videos live on Cloudflare R2 and are listed in the TV Promos collection.

<CaseSection id="broke">

**What broke:** every event page was showing the wrong date. It turned out dates were being saved at midnight UTC, and in Pacific time that’s still the evening before.

**What I changed:** event dates are now stored at midday, so they show on the right day.

**Also fixed:** repeat performers shared one page title in search results, so events now have their own SEO title field with the date in it (“Live Music: Sparrow Wren, Sept 19, 2026 | 14 Acres”), while the heading on the page stays undated.

</CaseSection>
